## 2026-10-19 overlap audit and stop gate

The published [EG-VAN paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390831641_EG-VAN_A_Global_and_Local_Attention_based_Dual-Branch_Ensemble_Network_with_Advanced_color_balancing_for_Multi-Class_Skin_Cancer_Recognition) describes seven HAM diagnoses plus a binary malignant/benign collection, and reports 1,332 original test images followed by augmented train and validation counts. It does not publish image assignments or a patient/lesion-aware split. Its table lists nine class names but does not establish numeric encoding. If a new protocol is approved later, use an explicitly adapted mapping `AKIEC=0, BCC=1, BKL=2, DF=3, MEL=4, NV=5, VASC=6, BENIGN=7, MALIGNANT=8`; do not call it a paper-specified index order. The binary source's published train/test organization is treated as protected here.

The read-only search emitted 41,381 candidate pairs at pHash **or** dHash Hamming distance ≤6: 37,015 binary–HAM and 4,366 binary–binary. This broad set contains false positives and is retained for review, not automatically grouped. More decisively, **1,926 binary–HAM pairs** have *both* 64-bit hashes identical (1,920 distinct binary files and 1,924 distinct HAM files). Resizing the corresponding HAM image to 224 × 224 with area interpolation gave mean absolute pixel difference <2/255 in 1,916 of those pairs and pixel correlation >0.99 in 1,925 pairs. Direct visual review of binary test `benign/1003.jpg` against HAM `ISIC_0028716.jpg`, and binary test `malignant/1003.jpg` against HAM `ISIC_0026847.jpg`, confirmed the same photographs; correlations after resizing exceeded 0.999. These results establish large cross-source re-encoded/resized overlap despite zero byte-identical cross-source hashes.

Among the strong binary–HAM pairs, HAM metadata labels are `nv` for 1,348 pairs and `mel` for 578 pairs. **401 distinct binary source-test images** have strong HAM matches. The same image would be labeled both `NV` and `BENIGN`, or both `MEL` and `MALIGNANT`, in the proposed nine-class target system. Those are different mutually exclusive targets, even though their broad clinical meanings may be compatible. Grouping them would prevent partition crossing, but would not resolve the target conflict or restore independent test cases.

There are also five binary–binary pairs with both hashes identical. Three are within train, including the byte-identical `malignant/768.jpg` and `769.jpg`. Two cross the published binary train/test directories: `test/benign/335.jpg` with `train/benign/1593.jpg`, and `test/malignant/507.jpg` with `train/malignant/439.jpg`. Visual inspection confirmed each pair depicts the same photograph; the second pair has pixel correlation >0.999. The source's own test boundary therefore contains near-duplicate leakage. Raw files were not changed.

### Paper label definitions and classification protocol

The [EG-VAN paper, §IV-A and Table 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390831641_EG-VAN_A_Global_and_Local_Attention_based_Dual-Branch_Ensemble_Network_with_Advanced_color_balancing_for_Multi-Class_Skin_Cancer_Recognition) combines HAM10000's seven diagnoses—actinic keratoses/intraepithelial carcinoma (AKIEC), basal cell carcinoma (BCC), benign keratosis-like lesions (BKL), dermatofibroma (DF), melanoma (MEL), melanocytic nevi (NV), and vascular lesions (VASC)—with a second collection's `benign` and `malignant` labels. Table 3 discusses all nine names. The class *names and broad combination* are paper-aligned; the paper does not provide a reproducible numeric class encoding, original image assignments, binary label derivation, overlap-resolution rule, or proof that this local Fanconi-like version is its exact second source. The mapping `AKIEC=0, BCC=1, BKL=2, DF=3, MEL=4, NV=5, VASC=6, BENIGN=7, MALIGNANT=8` is explicitly **local**.

In §IV-C, the paper says it first divided HAM into train/test with 1,332 original test images, augmented training data, then divided training into train/validation. It reports 37,883 training and 6,686 validation images *including augmented images*. It does not give the nine-class original-image split membership, binary source-test handling, random seed, or patient/lesion grouping. These counts and assignment cannot be reconstructed by assumption. A local adaptation should split originals before augmentation and leave validation/test deterministic.

### Scientific interpretation and recommended adaptation

HAM labels are diagnostic subtypes; binary labels are broad outcomes. They are nested concepts: a melanoma can also be called malignant and a nevus benign. A nine-way softmax therefore creates competing targets for the same clinical case. It also risks classifying acquisition/source artifacts (resolution, compression, colour, processing) instead of diagnosis. Report the result as a **source-conditioned nine-label research benchmark**, not a clinical nine-diagnosis taxonomy or calibrated malignancy predictor. Source-specific metrics and uncertainty must be disclosed. A hierarchical or multi-task clinical target would be a separate study.

For a *conditional, reduced-source, paper-aligned adaptation*, retain eligible HAM originals under their original `dx` labels and use only `data/isic/data/` for binary entries under their original folder labels. Never enumerate the mirror. Build a reviewed relation graph using SHA-256, pHash/dHash, normalized-pixel comparison, visual inspection where necessary, and genuine HAM lesion IDs. Exclude any binary entry **confirmed as the same photograph as a HAM image** from the nine-class sample, documenting both paths, evidence, reason, and retained HAM row; do not relabel HAM or invent binary metadata. Quarantine unresolved high-risk candidates pending review. Do not exclude or merge an unconfirmed image on perceptual similarity alone.

Apply the deterministic exact-content policy above: retain `train/malignant/768.jpg` and exclude `train/malignant/769.jpg` from sampling, without deleting either file. For the two confirmed binary train/test photo pairs, exclude the *train* counterpart (`train/benign/1593.jpg` and `train/malignant/439.jpg`) if the source-test counterpart remains eligible; otherwise document removal of both. Never assign a binary source-test image to training. If all 1,920 strong cross-source binary candidates are confirmed and excluded, at most 1,377 binary entries remain before the internal exact duplicate and any further near-match exclusions; this is **not** a final independent-case count.

Only after candidate review, exclusions, and provenance acceptance: create source-qualified metadata with original labels and available identifiers; group HAM records by verified lesion ID and all confirmed related photos by graph component; ensure no content group crosses splits. Preserve the binary source test as a protected boundary; allocate eligible HAM lesion groups and eligible binary source-train groups to train/validation/test using a frozen, seeded, class-stratified adapted rule. The earlier 80/10/10 target with seed 42 is a **proposed local choice**, subject to source-boundary and group constraints, not the paper's method. Report achieved counts and source-specific metrics. Keep final test untouched, validation/test transforms deterministic, and random augmentation training-only. Without binary patient IDs, patient independence cannot be claimed.

**Current gate:** a reduced nine-class task is feasible *in principle* with existing images, but no manifest or split is defensible until the overlap candidates are resolved and the reduced, source-conditioned target is accepted. Remaining decisions: accept the reduced binary sample and its task meaning; accept the local binary source's unverified version, license, and image-level labels for the intended claim; determine whether reviewed exclusions leave sufficient binary examples. No preprocessing, splitting, or training is authorized by this document.
