## Retinex and hair/color audit

The local 2025 IEEE Access article (DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3561240), article page 72856, prints Eq. (11) as `log(filtered image) - log(original RGB image)`. The Stage 4 [`retinex`](../src/preprocessing.py) follows that **printed sign**, then independently maps each channel's 1st–99th percentile to 0–255. The paper describes Gaussian scales and weights but does not fix their numeric values, a clipping/stretch rule, or an output display range. Standard reflectance Retinex often uses the opposite subtraction; the printed equation, prose and figure are insufficient to settle the authors' actual code. Therefore the sign is **not proven to be an implementation transcription error**. The subsequent per-channel stretch and sign can invert local bright/dark relations; treating the resulting JPEG appearance as clinically improved would be unjustified.

Stage 4 applies a 17-pixel elliptical **blackhat** mask, `response > 10`, then Telea radius 1; the paper's displayed hair morphology/threshold equations are not unambiguous and use a different top-hat/threshold notation. Masked lesion structures can also be inpainted. Gray World applies pairwise channel-mean gains in BGR storage order and clips to uint8. The three Retinex sigmas are 15, 80 and 250 with equal weights; logs use a `1e-6` floor; each channel is stretched independently. These numerical choices are local adaptations. OpenCV BGR is converted to RGB *after* processing, resized to 384×384, divided by 255, then ImageNet mean/std normalized. Only model-training images receive random flips/right-angle rotations. Fanconic JPEGs are already 224×224 with unknown upstream processing, so another color correction could compound unknown transformations; the same tonal reversal also occurs in HAM images and is not solely a Fanconic source effect.

## Bounded comparison and decision

[`src/nine_class_stage10_audit.py`](../src/nine_class_stage10_audit.py) deterministically considers the first 16 sorted **non-source-test** records per label, then selects the lowest brightness, highest brightness and highest dark-line-proxy case where distinct. This yielded 26 photographs across all nine labels: 21 HAM and 5 Fanconic. It compares A raw RGB resize; B hair removal plus Gray World; C the existing full Stage 4 pipeline; and D B plus an exploratory standard-sign luminance Retinex with sigma 80, strength 0.15 and gain clamped to 1/1.2–1.2. D uses no per-channel stretch and is **not claimed to reproduce the paper**. All four views use 384×384 resizing; model normalization is shared and therefore measured on display RGB before normalization. No protected source-test image enters the sample. Every selected source SHA-256 was checked before and after processing. The [sample ledger](../analysis/nine_class_stage10/descriptive_sample.csv), [104-row metric table](../analysis/nine_class_stage10/preprocessing_comparison.csv), [summary](../analysis/nine_class_stage10/preprocessing_summary.json) and [contact sheet](../analysis/nine_class_stage10/preprocessing_contact_sheet.jpg) record inputs, values and appearances. The audit refuses an existing output directory.

C had negative raw-view luminance correlation on **all 26** sampled images; source medians were −0.927 for HAM and −0.916 for Fanconic. The contact sheet visibly shows many pale lesion regions darkening or dark lesions lightening against the background. B/D also alter color substantially; the edge-correlation reduction may be affected by inpainting, channel gains and resampling. Histograms, brightness, color cast, clipping, sharpness and edge correlations are **descriptive proxies**, not proof that lesion detail or diagnosis is preserved. The 26 selected examples cannot estimate downstream accuracy or choose an optimized enhancement. The sample contains only five Fanconic examples, and its first-16 sampling frame is not representative of either source.

**Recommended candidate for future approved training:** [`raw_resize_imagenet_v2`](../config/nine_class_preprocessing_raw_v2.json): decode BGR, convert to RGB, resize to 384×384 with `INTER_AREA`, scale by 255, use the existing ImageNet RGB mean/std, and apply the existing random augmentation only in training. This is a conservative **adaptation** that avoids the observed tonal reversal; it does not assert superior accuracy. Retain B and D as optional candidates for a *train/validation-only* ablation after data approval, with C as an auditable paper-informed reference. Predeclare selection criteria and never use the protected source test to choose among them. The existing Stage 4 default remains `stage4_full_v1`; no historical checkpoint is changed.

The loader now accepts a frozen `pipeline_version` and its default's SHA-256 remains exactly the former Stage 4 hash `a55c731900a02428d7e1b9b4b27a32461ae84830f0780621bb1fb19c06fed9bc`. The raw candidate has distinct hash `93ea2577432511fd047f3d5a855d498aaabe63e6ff69094b5b758c16f85a3c68`. A future training configuration may set `"preprocessing_mode": "raw_resize_imagenet_v2"`; omitting it retains the old default. Stage 6 training records the complete hash in run provenance/checkpoints; resume, Stage 6 evaluation and Stage 9 external evaluation reconstruct the mode from the frozen run config and reject hash mismatches. Stage 9 internal/external comparisons also require matching hashes. The JSON candidate documents the mode but does **not** itself authorize training or alter the existing v1 training config.

## Split and system feasibility

The fixed index order remains `AKIEC=0, BCC=1, BKL=2, DF=3, MEL=4, NV=5, VASC=6, BENIGN=7, MALIGNANT=8`. Seven HAM diagnostic categories and two Fanconic broad folder labels overlap semantically; source can become a shortcut. They are nine **source-conditioned output labels**, not mutually exclusive clinical diagnoses. HAM labels were not mapped to Fanconic labels.

The existing Stage 6 protocol checks approved source rows, exact byte hashes and labels, duplicate SHA-256 groups, recorded/suspected overlap groups, available lesion/patient identifiers, source-test separation and all-nine class presence. Stage 4's helper is train/validation-only and cannot create a protected test. The 90 publisher-boundary graph crossings demand adjudication or quarantine. Publisher-test records cannot be moved into model training, and their linked HAM/Fanconic groups cannot cross into training while high-risk relations remain. BENIGN currently has **zero approved groups** for training, validation or test; all classes have zero approved groups. Thus a defensible final nine-class group-aware split is **not feasible now** despite the numerical upper bounds. No exploratory split was generated or treated as final.

Stage 6 training validates the approved cohort **before model construction** and also requires a frozen test containing all nine classes; it does not access the test images during optimization. Its v1 config remains unchanged and still defaults to Stage 4 preprocessing. Checkpoint/resume, internal evaluation, Stage 8 uncertainty and Stage 9 external evaluation remain subject to their frozen-cohort and matching-preprocessing gates. A GPU batch-size/memory plan has not been measured. Architecture software readiness does not establish data or GPU training readiness.
